# EUROPEAN-BANK-CHURN-RATE-ANALYSIS
About Dataset The European Bank Customer Churn Dataset contains customer-level information collected from a European retail bank. It is commonly used for binary classification tasks, where the objective is to predict whether a customer is likely to leave (churn) or remain with the bank.
European Bank Customer Churn Analysis
Overview
This data analyses 10,000 European bank customer records to identify factors associated with customer churn.

The analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel, including PivotTables, PivotCharts, correlation analysis, and an interactive dashboard.

Research Questions
Is customer age related to customer churn?
Is account balance related to customer churn?
How does the number of banking products affect churn?
Are inactive customers more likely to churn?
Does customer churn vary by geographical location?
Key Findings
Overall Churn Rate: 20.37%
Age vs Churn: Positive correlation of 0.2853, suggesting older customers have a higher tendency to churn.
Balance vs Churn: Very weak positive correlation of 0.1185.
Products: Customers with 2 products recorded the lowest churn rate at 7.58%.
Activity: Inactive customers had a 26.85% churn rate compared with 14.27% for active customers.
Geography: Germany recorded the highest churn rate at 32.44%, compared with Spain at 16.67% and France at 16.16%.
Recommendations
Target inactive customers with retention and engagement initiatives.
Develop retention strategies for older customers.
Prioritise high-balance customers for proactive relationship management.
Investigate the high churn among customers holding 3 or 4 bank products.
Review the factors contributing to the high churn rate in Germany.
